# ShowPoint Mission Research Context

Draft date: 2026-05-24  
Purpose: research-backed content blocks for a future website section or standalone page about why ShowPoint is intentionally local-first, narrow in scope, and careful with personal data.

## Working Preamble

The modern app economy has made a quiet bargain feel normal: use the tool, give up the data, accept the nudges. Personal information is often collected in the background, mixed with inferred behavior, and used to decide what people see, what they pay, what they are offered, and sometimes what they are denied.

AI makes that bargain harder to understand. It can turn ordinary traces - clicks, location, shopping history, browsing patterns, voice, images, and workplace activity - into predictions, segments, rankings, prices, and automated decisions. The harm is not only "someone has my data." The harm is that people lose visibility into how their lives are being shaped.

ShowPoint starts from a smaller, more respectful idea. A utility should do the job the user asked for, explain what it observes, and avoid turning behavior into a monetization engine. The cursor should become easier to see. The person using the computer should not become the product.

## User Pain Points

### Hidden Use of Personal Information

People often do not know what data is being collected, where it goes, how long it is kept, or whether it is sold, shared, inferred, or used later for a different purpose. Pew Research Center found in 2023 that 67% of Americans say they understand little to nothing about what companies do with their personal data, and 73% feel they have little or no control over data collected by companies.

Website angle:
"Most privacy harm starts before the breach: it starts when the user cannot see the deal they are being asked to accept."

### Manipulative Choice Architecture

Dark patterns are not only annoying checkout tricks. They can push people toward privacy-invasive settings, recurring charges, data sharing, or choices they would not make under clear conditions. The FTC's 2022 dark patterns report and the Norwegian Consumer Council's "Deceived by Design" report both frame interface design as a consumer autonomy problem, not just a UX preference.

Website angle:
"A button can be honest or it can be a trap. We design ours to be boringly honest."

### Surveillance Pricing and Personalization

The FTC's 2025 surveillance pricing study found that intermediaries can use data such as location, demographics, browsing patterns, shopping history, mouse movements, and abandoned-cart behavior to tailor prices, discounts, and product visibility. This turns ordinary activity into leverage over the buyer.

Website angle:
"When apps know more about you than you know about the price, the market stops feeling fair."

### Sensitive Location and Identity Data

FTC actions against data brokers such as Mobilewalla and Kochava show how location data can reveal visits to health clinics, places of worship, political gatherings, military installations, shelters, and other sensitive places. These are not abstract "data points"; they can expose beliefs, health status, family situations, vulnerability, and safety risks.

Website angle:
"The most sensitive data often looks harmless until it is connected to a person, a place, and a moment."

### AI-Expanded Risk

AI systems can increase the value and risk of collected data because they make inference cheaper. NIST's AI Risk Management Framework treats trustworthy AI as a matter of safety, security, accountability, privacy, and transparency. OECD's AI incidents work says risk monitoring matters because AI incidents are global, cross-sector, and difficult to compare without structured reporting.

Website angle:
"AI does not need perfect data to affect people. It only needs enough data to sort, score, predict, and persuade."

### Breaches and Secondary Harm

Even if a company says it will use data responsibly, stored personal data becomes a security liability. The ITRC's 2024 Data Breach Report notes nearly 12 billion victim notices across its tracked history. IBM's 2025 report found that 13% of studied organizations reported breaches of AI models or applications, and 60% of those AI-related security incidents led to compromised data.

Website angle:
"Data that is never collected cannot leak, be resold, be reidentified, or be repurposed."

## ShowPoint Positioning Principles

1. Do less, clearly.
ShowPoint should remain a focused utility. It makes the cursor, clicks, spotlight, and modifier state visible. It should not drift into recorder, editor, analytics, or behavior-profiling territory unless there is an overwhelming user benefit and a clear privacy model.

2. Collect the minimum.
The strongest privacy claim is not "we protect your data after collection." It is "we do not collect data we do not need."

3. Make observation legible.
If the app observes mouse position, click events, or modifier-key state, say exactly that. Avoid vague comfort language when precise language is more trustworthy.

4. Avoid manipulative monetization.
The product can charge money without turning the interface into pressure. Pricing, trial limits, Pro features, and upgrade prompts should be clear, symmetrical, and easy to dismiss.

5. Treat local-first as a design constraint.
No account, no analytics, no app-owned server, no screen recording, no typed-content capture. These are not marketing flourishes; they are product requirements.

6. Design for user agency.
The user should know what is happening, be able to turn it off, and never feel tricked into a privacy trade.

### Section 1: "The Problem Is Not Just Data Collection"

Body:
Apps increasingly turn personal information into predictions, rankings, prices, nudges, and automated decisions. AI accelerates this by making inference cheap and invisible. A click is no longer just a click when it can become a signal in a profile.

### Section 2: "Small Signals Can Become Sensitive"

Body:

A timeline of ordinary signals becoming inferred attributes: visit, search, pause, abandon cart, return, price changes.

### Section 3: "AI Makes The Data Economy More Opaque"

Body:

Visual idea:
Two-column contrast: "What the user sees" vs. "What the system may infer."

### Section 4: "Our Response: A Utility, Not A Funnel"

Body:
ShowPoint is intentionally narrow. It makes your cursor visible for meetings, demos, and tutorials. It does not record your screen, capture typed content, run analytics, require an account, or send behavioral data to a server.

Visual idea:
Checklist of what ShowPoint does and does not do.

### Section 5: "The Mission"

Body:
Software should help without quietly reshaping the person using it. ShowPoint exists because useful tools can be profitable without becoming surveillance systems.

Visual idea:
One strong line, no card:
"The cursor should be visible. The person should not be profiled."

## Short Copy Options

### Option A: Direct

The future of software should not require users to trade privacy for basic utility. ShowPoint is our small proof: a focused Mac app that helps people present clearly without accounts, analytics, screen recording, typed-content capture, or behavioral profiling.

### Option B: More Mission-Led

We are building against a pattern: apps that collect first, explain later, and monetize the gap between what users think is happening and what systems actually do. ShowPoint starts from the opposite premise. The tool should be useful. The data model should be obvious. The user should stay in control.

### Option C: Sharper

A cursor tool does not need to know who you are. It does not need a cloud profile, an analytics trail, a recorder, or a growth funnel. It needs to make the cursor visible and leave the rest of your life alone.

## Claims To Avoid Or Handle Carefully

- Avoid: "AI companies are stealing all data."  
  Better: "AI increases demand for data and makes inference cheaper, which raises privacy and accountability stakes."

- Avoid: "All personalization is manipulation."  
  Better: "Personalization becomes risky when it is opaque, hard to refuse, or used to exploit vulnerability."

- Avoid: "ShowPoint has zero network activity."  
  Better: "ShowPoint has no app-owned server or analytics. Purchases and restore flows go through Apple's App Store."

- Avoid: "No permissions needed" unless release validation remains true for the shipped build and macOS version.  
  Better: "No screen-recording permission. No typed-content capture. Permission copy should match clean-machine behavior."
